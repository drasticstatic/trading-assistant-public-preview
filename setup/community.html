The GEX Deep Dive

Their Asia-session read is nicknamed "night-vision" — which lands close to home, since our own ETH-derived ZTH levels in the auto-levels indicator (free reference guide: auto-levels.pine indicator guide) render green vs. cyan for exactly that reason. Years of building this system, and it's still finding new confluence to lean on

The underlying idea is gamma exposure (GEX): dealers who sell options have to hedge their own delta by trading the underlying, and the direction of that hedging flips depending on whether they're net long or net short gamma. Net long gamma, dealers hedge against the move — buying dips, selling rallies — which suppresses volatility and tends to pin price near the strikes with the heaviest open interest. Below the "zero gamma" level, that flips: dealers hedge with the move instead of against it, which is what turns an ordinary breakout into a fast, accelerating one. Put/call walls mark where that dealer positioning concentrates — not arbitrary technical levels, but the actual footprint of forced hedging flow, which is why price so often behaves differently above vs. below them

How We Got Here

It started on two machines at once — an iMac and a MacBook Pro, both running TradingView's Desktop App, trying to mirror the same trade by hand. TradingView doesn't allow multiple simultaneous sessions, so the MacBook side got pushed to a browser tab instead — which ran into the same wall from a different angle, since Tradovate was also logged into TradingView across desktop and mobile. Manual mirroring got paused for a while after that

Coming back to it meant simplifying to one machine, not two: TPT traded through TradingView's Desktop App, TopOne's BOGO accounts through a browser tab, both under the same login on the same iMac — switching Tradovate's account selector inside TradingView fast enough to never need a second tab. Three evals passed that way, each with multiple entries per account

Then TheGexLab needed its own tab, Lucid and Apex both came back into the mix, and leaving all of that to manual habit — on top of Starlink resync pauses and weather-related power outages that can take the whole trading terminal down — stopped feeling survivable. That's the real reason this scaled to TradeCopia's cloud syncing: not a nice-to-have, an enterprise-grade fix for an environment that isn't always stable. Lucid now runs as leader through the Desktop App, TopOne as leader through the browser, Tradovate's own UI still one click away either way — Fortuna's looped in on the plan now, and LittlebirdAI keeps quietly observing

TradingView's reverse-position button was also intentionally disabled to switch over to One-Click Trading instead — instantly placing, editing, canceling, or closing positions without a confirmation prompt — after watching TMade Alex run the same setup on DeepCharts

Maybe one day there's room for a Windows VM again, in order to have the opportunity to also run DeepCharts for order-flow data and lower latency — TradingView is known to get bloated under load with so many users on it, and both tools have real strengths worth having. For now, the time and attention goes to TheGexLab instead
