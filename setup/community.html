TradeCopia

Battle-tested — running the account structure today

Cloud-based, browser-driven — the upgrade from executing through multiple session instances of TradingView's native single-account-view switcher. Not just an easier, faster version of conducting clean business by hand: an enterprise-grade fix for a growing need — taking human error and lag out of manual mirroring, and moving off Tradovate's own "airplane cockpit" UI, which resets and lacks the drawing tools and TA that stay intact across every TradingView session

  • Hedge prevention — caps a follower's order at its existing size, never flips it into reverse
  • Position reconciler — corrects any follower fill that drifts from the leader, entry or exit
  • Auto-flatten the instant the leader goes flat — no stale bracket orders left behind

Running on TradeCopia's Pro+Lite tier here, not its local low-latency tier — the local-environment instability covered in the backstory above rules that one out, even though it's a feature a lot of people genuinely rely on. One real rough edge: live P&L doesn't always register the instant a position opens or closes, even though the pause/reconnect sounds fire right on cue — every trade so far has still executed exactly as configured regardless. Pro+ might smooth that over; just not an affordable upgrade yet, and the local tier still isn't a fit for laptops or a computer that goes to sleep mid-session

The Duck Race Story

A free Lucid futures account was won here via an on-stream duck race — first prize surfaced through community, not purchased. PropFirmMatchTV runs live-stream giveaways with a Discord + access-code flow, and this particular race is how Lucid ended up on the roster at all: a redemption code good toward a 25K account, style already decided in the account-structure plan above (25K follows, doesn't lead — see the comparison doc for the style analysis behind it)

Why it landed harder than "free 25K account": by July 20, a Lucid account had already been created specifically to dodge the 30-day eval-subscription clock TPT was running — this was around the time Apex Legacy accounts stopped being purchasable outright. Even so, a Lucid eval itself was never actually bought — TopOne's BOGO deal and TPT's 50AND3 offer had already covered enough ground. In a moment of prayer, weighing whether to even enter the giveaway at all while already juggling three funded accounts and two Apex renewals, the honest answer was: enough is enough for now — and that was said out loud, gratefully, as a letting go, not a complaint

Then the name got called. During Alex's hosting slot, using the code "tmade" — and hearing it live was genuinely a "wait, seriously?" moment, followed by tears. Not because of the account's dollar value, but because of how the whole journey had been unfolding up to that point. The gratitude here runs deeper than the outcome itself — it's encouragement, hope, and a real opportunity to level up and actually learn TradeCopia, scaling, and Lucid properly, rather than something owed or expected

Dave/MarketButcher and no shortage of other YouTube livestreamers in this space are affiliates too, running their own versions of these fun raffles and giveaway-style engagement while still mentoring — this duck race is one example of a much broader, genuinely fun pattern in this corner of YouTube

How We Got Here

It started on two machines at once — an iMac and a MacBook Pro, both running TradingView's Desktop App, trying to mirror the same trade by hand. TradingView doesn't allow multiple simultaneous sessions, so the MacBook side got pushed to a browser tab instead — which ran into the same wall from a different angle, since Tradovate was also logged into TradingView across desktop and mobile. Manual mirroring got paused for a while after that

Coming back to it meant simplifying to one machine, not two: TPT traded through TradingView's Desktop App, Top1's BOGO accounts through a browser tab, both under the same login on the same iMac — switching Tradovate's account selector inside TradingView fast enough to never need a second tab. Three evals passed that way, each with multiple entries per account

Then TheGexLab needed its own tab, Lucid and Apex both came back into the mix, and leaving all of that to manual habit — on top of Starlink resync pauses and weather-related power outages that can take the whole trading terminal down — stopped feeling survivable. That's the real reason this scaled to TradeCopia's cloud syncing: not a nice-to-have, an enterprise-grade fix for an environment that isn't always stable. Lucid now runs as leader through the Desktop App, Top1 as leader through the browser, Tradovate's own UI still one click away either way — Fortuna's looped in on the plan now, and LittlebirdAI keeps quietly observing

Somewhere in that same period, TradingView's reverse-position button got disabled entirely, switched over to One-Click Trading instead — instantly placing, editing, canceling, or closing positions without a confirmation prompt — after watching TMade Alex run the same setup on DeepCharts

Maybe one day there's room for a Windows VM again, just to properly test DeepCharts for order-flow data and lower latency — TradingView is known to get bloated under load with so many users on it, and both tools have real strengths worth having. For now, the time and attention goes to TheGexLab instead; screenshots of all this are coming through the TradeCopia-init session soon
